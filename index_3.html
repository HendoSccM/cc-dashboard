Incidents & Safety

1

Critical / Adverse Incident

WhenA fall, injury, ER visit, hospitalization, medication error, unsafe home condition, wandering, or death comes up on the call.

You getOne condensed paragraph with every pertinent fact, ending in the reportability flag, plus brief action items.

Prompt
TASK: The transcript contains a possible critical or adverse incident. Write a SINGLE condensed paragraph (5-8 sentences maximum) that captures all pertinent facts using ONLY what was stated in the conversation - no added details, no assumptions, no filler. The paragraph must cover, in natural narrative order: the type of incident; the date/time and location as stated (or note they were not stated); what happened, factually and chronologically; any injury or harm (or that none was reported); the immediate action taken and by whom; the member's current status; and who has already been notified (911, family, provider, physician), or that no notifications were reported. Omit anything not stated rather than padding. End the paragraph with this exact sentence: "This event may meet Critical Incident reporting criteria - route to a supervisor and file per SCCM's Critical Incident policy within the required timeframe."

Then add:
Action Items: A short bulleted list of clear next steps, including any HealtheFirst or GA Horizons updates.

If the transcript suggests possible abuse, neglect, or exploitation, do not analyze or conclude - add: "Possible APS concern - escalate immediately per SCCM APS and mandated-reporting policy."
Guardrail. This writes the note — it does not file the report. You still route and time it per SCCM's Critical Incident SOP. Never let the AI decide reportability; the flag is a cue to escalate.

APS Escalation Flag

WhenThe member or caregiver reports or hints at mistreatment — by an aide, agency, family member, or anyone — or financial exploitation or neglect.

You getA neutral, factual record to support immediate escalation to your supervisor.

Prompt
TASK: The transcript may contain a report of abuse, neglect, or exploitation. Document it factually and neutrally using these labels:

Reported Concern: What was said. Quote the member's or caregiver's exact words where possible; do not paraphrase the specifics away.
Who Was Involved (as stated): The person(s) named or described; write "unidentified" if not named.
When and Where (as stated): Timeframe and location, or "Not stated".
Member's Current Safety (as stated): Only what was said about the member's immediate safety.
Escalation Required: State exactly - "This report may be an APS (Adult Protective Services)-reportable situation. Escalate to a supervisor immediately and follow SCCM's APS and mandated-reporting policy. Do not investigate independently."

Do NOT use conclusion words like "abuse," "neglect," or "exploitation" as findings - write "the member reported..." or "the caregiver stated...". Do not interpret, minimize, explain away, or add reassurance or opinion.
Highest sensitivity. This supports escalation — it doesn't replace it. If a member is in immediate danger, that's a phone call, not a prompt. Tell your supervisor the moment you see it.

Provider & Agency Communication

3

Email to the Provider Agency

WhenA member shared an account you'd like the provider's perspective on, or you need to check something. What a member reports and what the provider's records show don't always match — we ask, we don't assume.

You getA kind, sincere, questions-led email to your provider partner.

Prompt
TASK: Draft a kind, sincere email from SCCM to the member's provider agency to better understand a situation raised in the transcript. We are in partnership with our providers, so the email must be warm, respectful, and lead with questions - never accusations or assumptions.

Important context: members sometimes share an account that turns out to be incomplete or inaccurate, and the provider may have documentation showing something different. The goal of this email is to invite the provider to shine light on the situation and share what they are seeing on their end.

Rules for the email:
- Open warmly and acknowledge the partnership.
- Describe what prompted the outreach factually and gently, using only what was said, and make clear we may not have the full picture.
- Lead with genuine questions: ask what the provider knows, whether they can help clarify, and what their records show.
- Minimum-necessary detail: refer to the member by first name and last initial or member ID - not full name, DOB, address, or diagnosis unless a specific detail is essential.
- Do NOT include a deadline or a requested response time.
- Keep the tone sincere, collaborative, and appreciative throughout.

Output exactly:
Subject: [warm, neutral subject line]
Body: [3-5 short paragraphs; leave the sender's name blank to fill in.]
Guardrail. We lead with questions, not conclusions. Read it once for tone and send only from your SCCM email. If it points to abuse, neglect, or exploitation, don't email — use the APS prompt instead.

Provider Complaint / Concern

WhenA complaint or concern about the provider that should be logged.

You getA complaint/concern record for GA Horizons, with provider outreach as the next step.

Prompt
TASK: The transcript contains a complaint or concern about the provider agency. This will be documented as a complaint/concern in GA Horizons. Document it using these labels, in paragraph form:

Nature of Complaint/Concern: What the member is unhappy about, factually.
Frequency / Pattern: How often, as stated, or "Not stated during call."
Impact on Member: The stated effect on the member's care or wellbeing.
Prior Resolution Attempts: Any steps already taken, as stated.
Member's Expectation: What the member wants to happen, as stated.
Action Items:
- Document this complaint/concern in GA Horizons.
- Reach out to the provider agency to make them aware of the concern, using a kind, questions-led approach (see the provider email prompt).
- Any other clear, owner-ready next steps stated in the call.
Guardrail. This becomes part of the GA Horizons record. Outreach is always kind and questions-led: we make them aware, we don't accuse.

Re-Broker (Change of Provider Agency)

WhenThe member is unhappy with their current provider agency and wants to switch to a new agency for services.

You getA detailed GA Horizons re-broker note with the required "why," plus the HealtheFirst and provider-search action items.

Prompt
TASK: The member is unhappy with their current provider agency and wants to switch to a new agency (a re-broker). Document it using these labels, in paragraph form:

Reason for Re-Broker: A detailed, specific explanation of why the member wants to change agencies, using only stated facts - what has happened, how often, the impact on the member, and any attempts already made to resolve it with the current provider. This section is required and must be thorough; do not summarize it into a single vague sentence.
Member's Expectation: What the member wants from a new agency, as stated.
New Provider Preference (as stated): Any agency the member named, or stated preferences (location, language, scheduling, services), or "Not stated during call."
Current Provider (as stated): The agency the member is leaving, or "Not stated during call."
Action Items:
- CA/CC: assist the member in identifying a new provider agency.
- Place a Re-Broker service in HealtheFirst.
- BOTH the Communications Analyst and the Care Coordinator: document this re-broker, including the detailed reason above, in GA Horizons.
- Any other clear, owner-ready next steps stated in the call.
Guardrail. The detailed "why" is not optional — a re-broker without a documented reason won't hold up in GA Horizons. And if the dissatisfaction involves possible abuse, neglect, or exploitation, route it through the APS prompt first — that's an escalation, not a provider switch.

6

Services On Hold / Suspension

WhenServices are pausing — hospitalization, rehab/SNF placement, member request, travel, or a safety concern.

You getA detailed GA Horizons note and a one-sentence EDWP provider notice, plus alerts for any missing detail.

Prompt
TASK: Services are being placed on hold or suspended based on the transcript. Produce TWO separate parts, plus a missing-information check.

First, identify the reason for the hold. Then check the Required Details for that reason below; for ANY required detail NOT stated in the conversation, do not guess - raise a clearly marked alert telling the CA/CC to obtain and add it.

Required details by reason:
- Hospitalization: hospital/facility name, admission date, expected discharge date, reason for admission.
- Rehab or SNF placement: facility name, admission date, expected return or discharge date.
- Member request: reason for the request, effective date of the hold, expected resume date.
- Travel: where the member is traveling, departure date, and expected return date (and a contact address while away, if services could resume there).
- Safety concern: nature of the concern, who identified it, effective date, and the plan or next step to resolve it.

Output exactly:

PART 1 - GA HORIZONS NOTE (detailed)
Reason for Hold: [one line]
Detailed Explanation: [a thorough, audit-defensible explanation of the reason for the hold and its circumstances, using only stated facts.]

** MISSING - ADD BEFORE FINALIZING **
[Bulleted list of each required detail not found in the conversation, written as an instruction - e.g., "Return date not stated: confirm with the member and add." If nothing is missing, write "All required details captured."]

PART 2 - EDWP PROVIDER NOTICE (one sentence only)
[Exactly ONE sentence to send to the provider, notifying them the member's services are being placed on hold. State the member (first name and last initial or member ID), the effective date if known, and the reason, plainly and professionally. No more than one sentence.]

Action Items: [bulleted - document Part 1 in GA Horizons, send Part 2 to the provider as the EDWP hold notice, update HealtheFirst, and obtain any flagged missing details.]
Guardrail. Part 1 goes in GA Horizons; Part 2 is the one-sentence EDWP notice to the provider. Don't finalize until every MISSING flag is cleared.

Change in Frequency Request

WhenThe call suggests the member may need more or fewer visits or hours.

You getA frequency-change request in the "Xhrs x Ydays wkly" format, for supervisor review.

Prompt
TASK: The transcript indicates a possible need to change the frequency of the member's services (more or fewer hours or days). Write a request suitable for supervisor review, capturing exactly how many hours are being requested. Tie it to the specific stated facts - functional changes, safety or fall risk, caregiver availability, or member goals - using only what was said. End by stating the request requires supervisor review.

Output exactly:
Requested Frequency: [state it in the format "Xhrs x Ydays wkly" - for example, "7hrs x 5days wkly". This is hours per day by days per week, NOT a total weekly hours figure. If the hours per day or the days per week was not stated, write "Not stated - confirm with member" for that piece.]
Current Frequency (if stated): [same "Xhrs x Ydays wkly" format, or "Not stated during call".]
Requested Change: [one sentence - e.g., "Increase from 5hrs x 5days wkly to 7hrs x 5days wkly."]
Reason for Request: [3-5 sentences tied to stated facts.]
Action Items: [route to supervisor for review; any HealtheFirst or GA Horizons steps.]
Guardrail. Express hours as hours-per-day by days-per-week (e.g., 7hrs x 5days wkly), never a weekly total. This is a request, not an approval — a supervisor reviews it before anything changes.

Deeper-Dive Topic Note

WhenOne topic needs more depth than the auto-note gave.

For an escalating complaint, a significant health change, a confusing or conflicting account, a sensitive family/caregiver situation, or anything a reviewer needs more depth on.

You getAn expanded, single-topic narrative pulled straight from the conversation.

Prompt
TASK: A single topic from the call needs more depth than the standard summary captured. The topic is:
[INSERT TOPIC - e.g., "the member's recurring falls in the bathroom" or "the ongoing dispute with the aide"]

Write an expanded narrative of 6-10 complete sentences, in third-person clinical language, covering only that topic. Pull every stated detail, put events in chronological order, include short exact member quotes where clearly stated, and note the implications for the member's care or safety as reflected in the conversation. Use "Not stated during call" for any gap a reader would expect filled.
Guardrail. Replace [INSERT TOPIC] every time — a vague topic gets a vague note.

Action-Items-Only Extract

WhenYou just need the to-do list, fast — no full write-up.

You getA clean, owner-ready task list.

Prompt
TASK: Extract only the action items from the transcript - nothing else. Return a bulleted list. For each item, state the action, the owner (Communications Analyst, Care Coordinator, provider agency, or member), and the due date or timeframe if stated. Where a change requires a system update, name the system (HealtheFirst or GA Horizons). If none are present, write "No action items identified during call."

Supervisor Handoff Note

WhenSomething needs your supervisor's attention beyond your scope. Used by both CAs and CCs.

You getA concise handoff to your supervisor with a clear ask.

Prompt
TASK: Something in the call needs your supervisor's attention beyond your scope. Write a concise handoff in third-person clinical language using these labels:

What Happened: The situation, factually.
Why It Needs the Supervisor: The reason it's beyond your scope.
The Ask: Exactly what you need your supervisor to do.
Guardrail. Be specific about the ask — a handoff without a clear request just moves confusion downstream.
